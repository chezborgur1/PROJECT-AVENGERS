### Data-source claims embedded in the plan (targeting signals)
- **"Google Maps data such as years open"** is **wrong as stated**. Places API (New) added an `openingDate` field on **Mar 17 2026**, but it gives the *anticipated* opening date for businesses not yet open. It is not a founding date or years in business. Use review count and oldest review date, Secretary of State formation date, or licence issue dates instead. Source: https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/release-notes ; https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/data-fields
- **Owner age** is not in Google, Secretary of State or most enrichment free tiers. It needs a paid people-data source or inference (e.g., licence issue year). Medium confidence; general knowledge, no single source found.
- **Google Maps Platform terms**: the Service Specific Terms let you cache place_id. Places lat/lng may be cached for at most 30 consecutive days (2025 versions). The main Maps Platform terms (no scraping, no caching beyond what is allowed) were not fetched directly. Scraper tools such as Outscraper sit outside Google's API terms. Source: https://cloud.google.com/archive/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms-20250501

## 3. Brokerage economics

- **Main Street commission norms:** ~**10%** is most common (MidStreet: "the majority of business brokers are charging 10%", range 8–10%). BizBuySell's article gives 10–15% up to ~$1M, with flat fees common under $100K. An IBBA range of 8–15% appears only second-hand. **Minimum fees** usually run **$10K–15K** (MidStreet, BizBen), and $10K–25K in other guides. Some franchise networks reportedly set minimums of $50K+. Double Lehman is used for larger deals (10% on the first $1M, stepping down to 2%). Dental practices: seller fee ~5–10% (some sources 6–12%). ## 4. Compliance and deliverability facts the engine must respect
- **CAN-SPAM penalty:** up to **$53,088 per violation**, the 2025 inflation-adjusted level. The FTC's 2026 notice (FR 2026-18853) keeps 2025 levels for 2026. Core duties: accurate headers and sender, non-deceptive subject, a valid physical postal address, a clear opt-out, and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. The 10-business-day rule is from the FTC CAN-SPAM guide (general knowledge; the FTC page was not fetched). Source: https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18853.pdf
- **Gmail bulk-sender rules** (≥5,000/day to Gmail): SPF, DKIM and DMARC; RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe for marketing mail; spam rate kept under 0.3%; unsubscribes processed within 2 days. Low-volume cold senders still benefit from all of these. Source: https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414
- **Google Workspace AUP** (Feb 9 2026 version) bans "mass unsolicited" promotional messages and allows suspension. **Workspace sending cap** is ~2,000 external recipients/day per paid user (~500 on trial or new accounts). Sources: https://cloud.google.com/archive/terms/aup-20260209 ; https://workspace.google.com/terms/use_policy.html ; https://mailreach.co/blog/google-workspace-email-sending-limits
- **Microsoft 365 TERRL:** since mid-Sept 2026, new tenants get **10% of quota in days 0–30, 25% in days 31–60**, and trial tenants 500 external recipients/day (MC1454397). A new M365 tenant would be throttled hard. Source: https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1454397 ; https://www.softcat.com/blog/microsoft-introduces-new-tenant-level-outbound-email-limit-exchange-online

## 5. Bottom line for the plan
- The **54% U.S. Bank stat, the BrokerPartnerz pricing, the BizBuySell $50 rate and the domain and mailbox prices hold up**. Add the dates, and describe BizBuySell's $50 as "introductory".
- **Fix:** ProspectOut's per-meeting price needs "+ ~$300/mo tools". The commission range needs "vendor estimate; ~$35K at the national median". "Free warm-up" should become "~$40–50/mo for a sending and warm-up tool". "Years open from Google Maps" is not available.
- **Timeline:** use ~4 weeks warm-up and 6–10 weeks to the first qualified seller, then 6–10 months from listing to close.

## Important facts the report missed

1. **CAN-SPAM requires cold commercial email to say it is an advertisement or solicitation** ("clear and conspicuous identification", 15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(A)), unless the recipient gave prior consent. Neither the plan nor the report lists this. Other requirements: the opt-out must keep working for at least 30 days after each send; the postal address may be a registered PO box or private mailbox; and AMB stays liable even if a vendor does the sending. *(prior knowledge)* **Design:** add a required disclosure line to every template, and lint templates for it.
2. **Checked directly: the Gmail connector cannot set custom headers or choose the "from" address.** `mcp__Gmail__send_message` and `mcp__Gmail__create_draft` accept only to, cc, bcc, subject, body, htmlBody, attachments, replyThreadId, replyToMessageId and includeSignature. That means:
   - **There is no way to add `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers.**
   - Mail always goes out from whichever account is authenticated. The connector must be linked to the separate sending-domain mailbox, not AMB's main inbox, and one connector covers one mailbox, which complicates rotating across 2–3 mailboxes.
   
   **Design:** either use an in-body opt-out link or "reply STOP" with a suppression list (enough for CAN-SPAM at low volume), or send through the Gmail API or a sending tool from the TypeScript code and use the connector only for reading and qualifying replies.
3. **Google Maps Platform terms likely block building a stored lead list from Places data.** The terms ban scraping, bulk download and caching beyond the allowed limits, and storing names, phone numbers and reviews in a Sheet or CRM would go beyond them; only place_id can be kept *(prior knowledge; check the current terms)*. Outscraper-style scraping breaks Google's terms outright. **Design:** keep only place_id plus your own derived fields, re-fetch details when needed, or source the list from Secretary of State filings and licence databases instead.
4. **California §10131 covers seller solicitation itself** (Row 23). This also applies to AMB's own outreach: it should send only to owners in the 1–2 target metros' states where AMB is licensed, or legally able, to take a listing and collect a fee.
5. **Microsoft Outlook.com consumer high-volume sender rules have applied since May 5 2025** (SPF, DKIM and DMARC for 5,000+ messages a day). The report covers only Gmail and Microsoft 365 tenants. *(prior knowledge)*
6. **AI-disclosure laws affect the AI that qualifies replies.** Utah's AI Policy Act (2024, amended 2025) requires disclosure when a consumer asks whether they are talking to AI. Other states have bills in progress. *(prior knowledge; check the current scope)* **Design:** when anyone asks, the qualifier must answer truthfully that it is AI, and the sender should be a real, named person at AMB.
7. **"Years owned" is not the same as "years since formation"**, and owner age has no free source. Buying owner age from people-search data brokers can bring in state data-broker and privacy rules. Treat both as optional, inferred signals with a confidence score.
8. **Every number dated after June 2026 is open**: BizBuySell Q2 2026, IBBA Q1/Q2 2026, Verisign Nov 2026, Microsoft MC1454397, Places `openingDate`, FR 2026-18853 and the 2026 benchmark reports. Neither the researcher nor I could load these pages, so check them live before they reach client-facing copy or ROI figures.
