# Building a likely-to-sell owner list legally and cheaply (US Main Street verticals)

Research for AMB Advisory Services' seller lead engine. Date: 2026-10-18.

**Method and limits.** The sandbox's network proxy blocked WebFetch and curl to every target host (DNS failures and HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so I could not open any page directly. Every fact below comes from WebSearch result excerpts of the cited pages. Where the excerpts did not settle a point (exact CSV header names, some prices), I say so. The search budget ran out before I could check the CAN-SPAM opt-out timing. Before the engine depends on a file, it should read that file's real header row and the live terms page.

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Build the base list from government licence files, not Google.** The best free, bulk, legal sources that name people and give dates are:
   - California CSLB (contractors, with a personnel file)
   - Texas TDLR (A/C contractors, daily CSV)
   - Florida DBPR (construction, weekly CSV, original licensure date)
   - Texas TSBDE (dentists, daily CSV with original issue date and year graduated)
   - Washington L&I (contractor principals)
   - CMS NPPES (dentist organisations, with an authorised official and an enumeration date)

   Then join state entity files on name and address: Florida Sunbiz has officers, Colorado and Oregon do not. Most of these are free CSV or fixed-width downloads.
2. **Use Google Places only as transient enrichment, never as the list source.** The Maps Platform Terms ban scraping and exporting content for use outside the Services, and ban caching except where the terms allow it. Only `place_id` may be stored indefinitely. There is no "years open" field.
3. **Do not use voter files or data-broker age appends.** Most states ban commercial use of voter files. Use proxies for owner age instead: years since the original licence was issued, years since the entity was formed, and for dentists, year of graduation.
4. **Email discovery.** Start with the business's own website (contact page, staff and about pages). Then use Hunter, Snov and Apollo free tiers sparingly (each is about 50 credits a month), and verify every address through a paid or free-tier verifier API. Keep hard bounces under 2% per mailbox and treat catch-all domains as risky.
5. **Prioritise verticals with Census data.** County Business Patterns gives establishment counts by NAICS code and county. The Annual Business Survey gives owner age (variable OWNRAGE) by sector. The latest national figure is that 51.3% of employer-firm owners are 55 or older (2023 ABS, via secondary sources).
6. **Legal flag for the "sell leads to other brokers" idea.** Selling records about owners could make AMB a registered "data broker" in California, Texas, Vermont and Oregon. That brings registration fees and, in California, DROP deletion processing, mandatory since 2026-08-01. The CCPA's B2B exemption ended on 2023-01-01.

## 3. Google Maps / Places API (New)

**Terms of Service**
- **No scraping.** Maps Platform ToS §3.2.3(a) bars exporting, extracting or scraping Google Maps Content for use outside the Services. The examples include bulk-downloading places data and copying business names, addresses or reviews. [Maps Platform ToS](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms), [ConductAtlas summary](https://conductatlas.com/platform/google-maps/google-maps-platform-terms-of-service/no-scraping-or-content-extraction/). Google enforces this with temporary API restrictions and warnings of suspension. In one shared notice, it said building databases or keeping permanent copies is prohibited. [Bubble forum](https://forum.bubble.io/t/google-maps-violation/194549)
- **No caching** unless the Service Specific Terms allow it (§3.2.3(b)). **Place IDs are exempt and may be stored indefinitely.** Google recommends refreshing a stored place ID after 12 months, and a Place Details call asking only for the ID costs nothing. [Places policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies), [Place IDs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/place-id). **Latitude and longitude may be cached for up to 30 consecutive days.** [Service Specific Terms (Apr 2024)](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms/index-20240422), [May 2025 archive](https://cloud.google.com/archive/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms-20250501). Ratings, phone numbers, websites and review counts have **no** caching exception. [openplacesapi](https://openplacesapi.com/blog/can-you-store-places-api-results)
- Other regional terms (Spanish-language and EEA versions) add AI/ML training bans, and the Grounding product bans scraping outright. Don't feed Places content into model fine-tuning. [Search excerpt of terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)

**Fields and SKUs** (billing follows the highest-tier field in the FieldMask, and a request with no mask errors) [Usage & billing](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/usage-and-billing), [Text Search docs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/text-search)
- `businessStatus`: **Pro**. [Data fields](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/android-sdk/data-fields?authuser=6)
- `rating`, `userRatingCount`, `websiteUri`, `internationalPhoneNumber`, `regularOpeningHours`: **Enterprise**. Reviews: **Enterprise + Atmosphere**. `types` / `primaryType` are lower tier (not separately verified).
- **No "years open" or founding-year field.** `openingDate` (added 2026-03-17) is only the *expected* opening date for businesses that have not opened yet. [Release notes](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/release-notes)
- Text Search (New) returns at most 20 results per page and **60 per query**, so city-wide discovery needs a grid of queries. [searchText ref](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/reference/rest/v1/places/searchText)

**2026 pricing** (from the official price list excerpt; volume tiers decrease from there) [Pricing list](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)
- The $200 monthly credit was replaced in **March 2025** by free caps for each SKU (not pooled across SKUs): Essentials 10,000, Pro 5,000, Enterprise 1,000 per month. [Woosmap](https://www.woosmap.com/blog/google-maps-api-pricing-breakdown), [overview](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/overview)
- **Text Search / Place Details Essentials ID-only: unlimited and free.**
- Text Search Pro: $32 per 1,000 after 5,000 free. Text Search Enterprise: $35 (1,000 free). Enterprise + Atmosphere: $40.
- Place Details Pro: $17 (5,000 free). Place Details Enterprise: $20 (1,000 free). Enterprise + Atmosphere: $25.
- Monthly subscription plans (from about $100/month) are reported by a third party ([Woosmap](https://www.woosmap.com/blog/google-maps-api-pricing-breakdown)); not verified.

**Engine design implication.** Match each licence or entity record with a free **ID-only Text Search** (name + address) and persist only `place_id` plus a match confidence. At scoring time, call **Place Details Enterprise** for rating, userRatingCount, businessStatus and websiteUri. That is 1,000 free calls a month, enough for the first 500 owners at $0. Use the values in memory, or in a cache that is purged automatically within 30 days at most. Whether storing derived scores is allowed is a gray area and should go to counsel. For persisted category, website and phone data, use open POI datasets:
- **Overture Maps Places** (64M+ places; mostly CDLA-Permissive-2.0, some CC0 / Apache-2.0). `categories` was deprecated in favour of `basic_category` / `taxonomy` and was due for removal in the September 2026 release. [Overture guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places), [2026-05-20 notes](https://docs.overturemaps.org/blog/2026/05/20/release-notes/)
- **Foursquare OS Places** (Apache-2.0, monthly). It has `date_refreshed` and `date_closed`; I could not confirm a `date_created` field. [FSQ schema](https://docs.foursquare.com/data-products/docs/places-os-data-schema), [ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/docs/getting-started/example-datasets/foursquare-places)

Third-party "Google Maps scrapers" (Apify, Outscraper and similar) violate Google's ToS for the content they take, so don't use them.

## 4. Owner-age signals: what is legal and ethical

**Avoid**
- **Voter files.** The EAC says even the least restrictive states prohibit commercial use of voter file data. [EAC Voter Roll Privacy](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf). Montana Free Press counts 37 states that expressly ban commercial use ([MTFP](https://montanafreepress.org/?p=51041)). Ballotpedia (July 2026) finds that in 21 states plus DC, commercial use is the only restriction ([Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/24/twenty-one-states-and-d-c-have-open-availability-of-voter-files/)). Penalties include a Kansas class C misdemeanor, an Iowa serious misdemeanor and a New Mexico fourth-degree felony. **Do not use them.**
- **Data-broker age or date-of-birth appends.** California's Delete Act DROP lets residents send one deletion request to every registered broker. Brokers had to start processing requests on **2026-08-01**, checking at least every 45 days. Penalties are cited as $200 per request per day. [CPPA DROP](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop-for-data-brokers/), [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publication/dont-drop-ball-upcoming-changes-california-delete-act-five-key-steps-companies). Registration laws also exist in Texas (Bus. & Com. Code ch. 510, $300 fee, annual) [TX SOS](https://www.sos.texas.gov/statdoc/data-brokers.shtml), Vermont and Oregon [Galalaw](https://blog.galalaw.com/post/102ituk/u-s-data-broker-legislation-expands-to-include-texas-and-oregon). Broker age data is also often inferred and inaccurate, and using it in outreach would read as creepy.
- **CCPA.** The B2B contact exemption expired on 2023-01-01, so business-contact data about California owners is personal information. Whether it applies to AMB depends on the CCPA thresholds. My understanding is roughly $25M+ revenue (CPI-adjusted), 100k+ consumers, or 50%+ of revenue from selling data; I did not verify that. [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/californias-looming-privacy-deadline-for-personnel-and-b2b-data/)
- **If AMB sells leads to other brokers**, it may itself meet the definition of a "data broker" in CA, TX, VT and OR. Texas 2025 amendments may broaden coverage. [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/publication/data-broker-rules-regulators-catch-businesses-off-guard/). Get counsel before launching that line of business.

**Use these proxies** (public professional records, used for business purposes, never mentioned in outreach)
1. **Years since the original licence was issued** (CSLB, DBPR, TDLR, WA L&I). Contractor licensure usually needs years of prior experience, so estimated age ≈ 28–30 + years licensed. This is my heuristic: low precision, but directional.
2. **Dental school graduation year** (TSBDE): estimated age ≈ (current year − graduation year) + 26. This is my heuristic; most DDS/DMD graduates are about 25–27.
3. **Years since entity formation** (Sunbiz, CO, NY, OR, TX).
4. **Officer stability** (Sunbiz annual-report officers). The same sole officer for 15+ years, with no officer added who shares the owner's surname, suggests no family successor.
5. Website text: "since 1985", "family owned for 3 generations" (positive or negative), and an old copyright year in the footer.

## 5. Email discovery and verification

| Tool | Free tier (2026) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hunter | **50 credits/month** shared across Finder, Verifier and Domain Search (1 credit per find, 0.5 per verification). Starter $49/month. | [hunter.io/pricing](https://hunter.io/pricing), [help](https://help.hunter.io/en/articles/11131690-pricing-plans-and-feature-faqs) |

| Snov.io | 50 credits and 100 recipients/month, warm-up for 1 mailbox; no bulk search or verify on free | [Snov KB](https://snov.io/knowledgebase/?p=15063), [Emelia](https://emelia.io/hub/snov-io-pricing) |
| ZeroBounce | 100 verifications/month (third party) | [startuphub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-tools/2026/best-zerobounce-alternatives-email-verification) |
| MillionVerifier | 100 free once; about $89 for 50k credits that never expire (third party) | [syncgtm](https://syncgtm.com/blog/millionverifier-review-2026) |

- **Volume reality.** The free tiers add up to roughly 150–200 finds a month, which is short of 500 owners. The primary path should be: crawl the business's own site (`/contact`, `/about`, `/team`, mailto links), then guess patterns (first@, firstlast@), then verify. Owner first and last names come from the licence and SOS files.
- **SMTP verification.** The method is: MX lookup → connect on port 25 → HELO, MAIL FROM, RCPT TO → QUIT, sending no message. Probe a random address to detect catch-all domains. Many hosts block outbound port 25 (DigitalOcean does for new accounts), and bursts of RCPT probes get IPs flagged. [DigitalOcean Q&A](https://digitalocean.com/community/questions/smtp-handshake-not-working-blocked-on-port-25), [verifier notes](https://claudemarket.ai/skills/psyduckler/email-verifier). **Implication:** a Claude-run cloud sandbox should call a verifier API rather than open SMTP connections itself.
- **Thresholds**
  - Hard bounce under 2%: the industry benchmark, per [Hunter](https://hunter.io/cold-email-guide/email-bounce-rate).
  - 2–4% is a warning zone ([Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-email-bounce-rate)). A 3–5% stop rule is one vendor's internal policy ([Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/explore/cut-cold-email-bounce-rate)).
  - Recommendation: **auto-pause a mailbox above 3%** over its last 100 or more sends.
  - Gmail: keep the spam rate **below 0.1% and never at 0.3%** ([Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414?hl=en)). Senders above 0.3% are ineligible for mitigation, and enforcement ramped up from November 2025. Bulk-sender rules apply at about 5,000 messages a day ([Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/compliance/how-does-google-postmaster-compliance-work-and-what-are-the-volume-thresholds-for-bulk-senders)).
- **Catch-all domains** are typically 15–30% of B2B lists and bounce far more often (one vendor test, cited second-hand, says about 27× verified addresses). Score them or cap their share rather than mailing them freely. [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/catch-all-email-addresses), [BounceZero](https://bouncezero.io/email-bounce-prevention-guide-2026)

## 7. What predicts a sale (evidence behind the score)

- **Retirement is the top reason owners sell.** BizBuySell's Q2 2026 owner survey, via a secondary source: retirement 45%, new opportunity 29%, burnout 21%, economic uncertainty 13%. [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/why-owners-sell-their-businesses). In Q1 2022 it was 55% retirement. [SmallBizTrends](https://smallbiztrends.com/2022/04/bizbuysell-2022-quarter-1-small-business-sales-report.html/email/)
- **Seller age.** A broker reports the average seller age moving from about 65 to about 70, with health often the trigger (2022, anecdotal). [Calder](https://caldergr.com/retirement-reason-small-business-sale/)
- **Succession gap.** U.S. Bank 2025: only **54%** have a formal succession plan; 62% find succession overwhelming; 56% fear a low price; 37% plan to sell within 12 months. This confirms the plan's 54% claim. [U.S. Bank](https://www.usbank.com/about-us-bank/company-blog/article-library/us-bank-survey-looks-at-small-business-stressors-ai-and-succession-plans.html), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250611019480/en/)
- **Tenure is not linear.** Holmes and Schmitz (J. Labor Econ., 1996, Census CBO data) find the odds of sale or failure *decrease* with manager tenure at first. Tenure alone is therefore a weak predictor. It matters mainly as a proxy for age and retirement. [UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/managerial-tenure-business-age-and-small-business-turnover/)
- **No family successor.** Family firms plan outside sales less often (44% vs 75% for non-family firms in an Australian survey), and about 1 in 5 planned sales cite the lack of a family successor. [search summary](https://woodgundyadvisors.cibc.com/delegate/services/file/33310546/content)
- **Planning horizon.** In Colorado, 52% of owners plan to transition within 5 years, and 68% have spent minimal time on exit planning (EPI 2022). [CO SOOR](https://oedit.colorado.gov/sites/coedit/files/documents/2022%20Colorado%20SOOR%20Report.pdf)

## 8. Recommended data-source stack (first 500 owners, about $0 in data cost)

1. **Vertical and metro choice:** CBP plus ABS OWNRAGE, which gives `vertical_prior.csv`.
2. **Base list, depending on the target state:**
   - HVAC/plumbing: CSLB (CA), TDLR ACR (TX), DBPR CILB (FL) or WA L&I principals.
   - Dental: TSBDE (TX), plus NPPES organisation records nationwide.
   - Landscaping, cleaning, property management: SOS entity files (Sunbiz with officers, CO, OR, NY), filtered by name keywords and formation date at least 15 years ago.

   **The best first pilot is Florida** (Sunbiz officers plus DBPR original licensure date) **or Texas dental** (TSBDE graduation year plus NPPES).
3. **Entity join:** match licence business name and address to SOS records (formation date, officers).
4. **Places enrichment:** free ID-only match, then Enterprise Details within the 1,000/month free cap at scoring time; persist only `place_id`. Use Overture or FSQ for persisted website, phone and category.
5. **Contacts:** crawl the business site, then Hunter, Snov and Apollo free credits, then verify with a verifier API.
6. **Suppression:** a global opt-out list, plus a check that the business is not franchised or PE-owned.

### CSV import formats the engine should accept

All files: UTF-8, comma-delimited, header row, ISO dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Each importer has an adapter that maps raw state headers to these canonical names. Each row carries `source`, `source_record_id` and `source_snapshot_date` for provenance.

**`entities.csv`** (SOS)
`source, source_record_id, state, entity_name, entity_type, status, formation_date, principal_street, principal_city, principal_state, principal_zip, mailing_street, mailing_city, mailing_state, mailing_zip, registered_agent_name, registered_agent_type, officer1_name, officer1_title, officer2_name, officer2_title, … officer6_name, officer6_title, last_annual_report_date, source_snapshot_date`

**`licenses.csv`** (trade or professional boards, NPPES)
`source, license_number, license_type, license_class, status, business_name, dba_name, person_name, person_role (owner|qualifier|officer|principal|authorized_official|licensee), original_issue_date, effective_date, expiration_date, year_graduated, school, practice_description, street, city, state, zip, county, phone, npi, taxonomy_code, source_snapshot_date`

**`places_links.csv`** (the only Google data that is persisted)
`lead_id, place_id, match_method (id_only_text_search), match_confidence (0-1), place_id_checked_at`
Rating, review count, status and website are *not* imported. They are fetched live into a cache that expires within 30 days at most.

**`open_poi.csv`** (Overture / FSQ)
`source, poi_id, name, basic_category, website, phone, street, city, state, zip, lat, lng, date_refreshed, date_closed, license (CDLA-P-2.0|Apache-2.0|CC0)`

**`contacts.csv`**
`lead_id, first_name, last_name, role, email, email_source (website|hunter|snov|apollo|pattern|manual), email_source_url, verification_status (valid|invalid|catch_all|unknown|role_based|disposable), verifier, verified_at`

**`suppression.csv`**
`value, value_type (email|domain|business_id), reason (opt_out|bounce|complaint|customer|do_not_contact), added_at`

**`vertical_prior.csv`**
`naics, sector_naics2, state, cbsa_or_county_fips, establishments, employees, share_owners_55_plus, prior_weight, census_vintage`

## 9. Likely-to-sell scoring model (0–100; heuristic, to be recalibrated)

The weights below are my starting heuristic, grounded in the research above. They are not fitted to data. Once the engine has about 50 or more replies labelled qualified or not, refit the weights with a logistic regression.

| Component | Weight | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimated owner age** | 30 | From graduation year (dentists), else licence issue year + 28, else formation year + 30, keeping the most confident estimate. Under 45 → 0; 45–54 → 10; 55–59 → 20; 60–64 → 27; 65+ → 30. Store `age_est_method` and `age_confidence`. |
| **Tenure** (years since the earliest of formation, original licence or NPI enumeration) | 20 | Under 10 → 0; 10–14 → 6; 15–19 → 12; 20–24 → 16; 25+ → 20. Kept below age because of Holmes & Schmitz. |
| **No apparent successor** | 15 | Sole officer, principal or qualifier for 10+ years → 15. Several officers but none sharing the owner's surname → 8. An officer sharing the surname with a different first name (likely next generation) → 0. Website says "2nd/3rd generation" → 0. |
| **Size and sellability fit** | 15 | userRatingCount (live), number of licence classes, employee band where known. Target the plan's range (roughly $500K–$5M revenue). A tiny one-truck or solo operation, or something over about $10M, scores low. |
| **Fatigue / transition signals** | 10 | Footer copyright 5+ years old; no replies to Google reviews (live check); qualifier recently changed; late annual report; licence near expiry and not renewed early. |
| **Vertical and metro prior** | 10 | From `vertical_prior`: share of owners 55+ and buyer demand. |
| **Knock-outs** (score = 0) | – | businessStatus CLOSED_PERMANENTLY, an inactive entity or licence, franchise or PE / platform-owned, on the suppression list, or no verified owner email (send to manual review). |

Tiers: **A ≥ 70** (send first, personalise heavily), **B 50–69**, **C under 50** (hold).

Never mention age, health, family or succession assumptions in emails. Frame the offer as the free exit-readiness check. Log every score component so the AI reply-qualifier can feed outcomes back for recalibration.

## Important facts the report missed

1. **The Claude cloud network allowlist (seen in this session).** Scheduled Claude cloud tasks run behind an HTTPS-only allowlist proxy. Websites, verifier APIs, state data portals and Google APIs were refused or failed DNS. The engine needs its egress hosts allowlisted in the environment's network settings, or the fetch, crawl and verify steps must run somewhere else (for example a small VM or GitHub Actions) with Claude working on the results.
2. **CAN-SPAM aggravated violations (15 U.S.C. §7704(b)(1)).** Two practices in the report are covered:
   - **Dictionary attacks**: generating addresses by "combining names, letters, or numbers into numerous permutations." The report's first@ / firstlast@ guessing path is close to this. Limit it to one or two patterns per named person, verify before sending, and log the method.
   - **Harvesting** from sites that say they don't give out addresses. The site crawler must respect such notices and robots.txt.
   Both raise penalties and can bring criminal exposure (18 U.S.C. §1037).
3. **CAN-SPAM details the report skipped:** the advertisement-identification requirement, the 30-day opt-out window, no conditions on opting out, the rule that both the sender and the "initiator" are liable, and the yearly penalty adjustment.
4. **The ID-only Places match returns no name or address.** Fixing the match needs paid-tier fields (see fact 15).
5. **Licence holders are often qualifiers, not owners.** This applies to CSLB RMEs, TDLR individual licensees and DBPR qualifiers. The schema needs `person_role` confidence and an owner-confirmation step: the AI qualifier should confirm ownership in the first reply.
6. **The CCPA "business" thresholds decide whether CA data-broker rules apply at all.** A small AMB that sells a few hundred leads probably isn't a CA data broker until lead sales reach 50% of revenue. Texas adds a surety bond. Most other state privacy laws exempt people acting in a B2B or employment role; California does not.
7. **No federal beneficial-ownership data.** FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule removed reporting by domestic companies under the Corporate Transparency Act, and BOI was never public. NY LLCTA data is also non-public. Owner names must come from licence and SOS files and from the owner's own replies.
8. **Google Workspace and the Gmail connector.** As I recall, Workspace's Acceptable Use Policy prohibits sending spam, including email that breaks CAN-SPAM, and there are daily sending caps (about 2,000/user/day; trial accounts are lower). Workspace prices rose in 2025, which may push the plan's $7/mailbox figure. Before mailboxes are bought, check that low-volume, compliant, one-to-one cold email through the connector stays within the AUP and caps.
9. **FSQ OS Places has `email`, `tel`, `website` and `date_created`.** It is a free, Apache-2.0 contact and enrichment source that can be stored permanently.
10. **Per-source use restrictions.** Some state licence boards attach "no solicitation" or commercial-use terms to bulk lists. Record the terms URL and date per source in `source_snapshot` and check them before import.
11. **Dental-specific knock-outs.** Flag DSO-affiliated practices; NPPES organisation names and shared Authorized Officials across many NPIs are signals. Note that many dental sales go through dental-specialist transition brokers, so the competition and the buyer pool differ from HVAC or landscaping.
12. **Texas HVAC may have no issue-date field.** For TX HVAC, tenure may have to come from the Comptroller dates or the Sales Tax permit "first sales date" (data.texas.gov; unverified).
